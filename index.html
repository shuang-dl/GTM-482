Executive Summary

Bottom line: Fin AI's CSAT problem is primarily an audience problem — 90% of callers are tenants and non-customers Fin was never designed to serve. And the June "0% CSAT" shown in the report is a data artifact, not a real signal.

After pulling all 2,368 Fin Voice conversations (Jan–Jun 2026), two structural facts dominate: (1) 2,133 of 2,368 callers (90.1%) are classified as "leads" — tenants, rental applicants, or unrecognized numbers — not DoorLoop property managers. Every single one of the 9 low-rated calls came from a lead caller, not a user. (2) The June "0% Fin AI CSAT" in the Intercom report is a metric definition artifact — the ai_agent.rating field had zero entries in June because Fin resolved no calls solo that month; the 4 actual June ratings that exist are all 5/5. Beyond those structural findings, three content-level failure patterns emerge from transcript review: login/access blockers where the fix requires PM action Fin can't take; refund/fee calls from applicants expecting DoorLoop authority it doesn't have; and "Confirmed Resolution" being overclaimed on calls that ended unresolved.

Why does the report show 0%? The Intercom report's "Fin AI Agent CSAT" metric uses the ai_agent.rating field specifically — which only populates when Fin resolves a call end-to-end with no escalation. In June, zero calls have an ai_agent.rating value. The report interprets this as 0%, when the correct reading is no data — Fin didn't resolve any calls solo in June in a way that triggered that rating field. The four 5/5 ratings that do exist in June are stored in conversation_rating, which tracks the overall call, not Fin's specific contribution. This is a metric definition gap, not a crisis.

Why this matters for CSAT

Every single low-rated call (all 9 of them) came from a "lead" caller — a tenant, applicant, or unrecognized number. Not one 1-rating came from an actual DoorLoop customer. This means Fin is not failing property managers — it's being called by people it was never designed to serve, and CSAT is being dragged down by that mismatch. The structural fix is audience routing upstream, not prompt tuning downstream.

Top 3 Failure Patterns — Fin AI Resolved Bucket (65%)
Failure Pattern #1
🔐 Login / Access Blocker — Fin Can't Help Tenants
Frequency: 2 of 5 calls (40%)

What's happening: Tenants call with login errors — wrong email, unrecognized password, unaccepted portal invite. Fin correctly troubleshoots but hits a wall: the fix (resending the portal invite, confirming the email address) requires the property manager to act in the dashboard. Fin can't do this, and neither can the tenant on the phone.

Why customers rate 1/5: They called to fix a problem and hung up still locked out. "Call your property manager" is not a resolution — it's a redirect. But Fin marks it as "Confirmed Resolution."

The core issue: Fin is calibrated to confirm resolution when the user acknowledges the next step, not when the problem is actually solved. This is a resolution-state logic flaw.

🔧 Recommended Fixes
  • Add a Fin Procedure: If caller is a tenant who cannot log in and the fix requires PM action → do NOT mark as resolved; route to "warm transfer" or log a ticket with PM notification
  • Update resolution logic: "User acknowledged next step" ≠ "problem solved." Require Fin to ask "Were you able to log in successfully?" before confirming resolution on login topics
  • Build a Custom Scorecard for tenant login calls: flag any call where resolution = "contact your property manager" as CSAT risk
Failure Pattern #2
💰 Wrong Audience — Tenant/Applicant Calls Fin Can't Own
Frequency: 1–2 of 5 calls (20–40%)

What's happening: Callers who are tenants, co-signers, or rental applicants reach Fin expecting DoorLoop to have authority over things it doesn't control: application fee refunds, leasing decisions, Zillow listing setups. Fin accurately explains DoorLoop's limits ("we don't process refunds, contact the property owner") but this feels like a dead end to frustrated customers.

Why customers rate 1/5: They believe DoorLoop owes them something (a refund, a solution, access to a human). Fin's technically correct answer — "contact the property manager" — lands as dismissive, especially when the customer is emotionally charged.

Structural root cause: These callers shouldn't be reaching Fin Voice at all. They are prospects or tenants, not DoorLoop customers. Fin has no ability to take action on their behalf.

🔧 Recommended Fixes
  • Add an early caller identification step to the voice workflow: "Are you a DoorLoop customer (property manager/owner), or are you a tenant or rental applicant?" — route non-customers differently
  • For application fee / refund calls: add a Fin Procedure with empathy language before the policy statement, and offer to send a follow-up email with landlord contact info
  • Flag calls categorized as "Rental Applications" with no DoorLoop user match as CSAT risk / wrong-audience calls for review
Failure Pattern #3
📊 "Confirmed Resolution" Overclaiming — CSAT Validity Issue
Frequency: 4 of 5 calls (80%) — cross-cutting

What's happening: Intercom labels all 5 of these calls as ai_agent_resolution_state: confirmed_resolution — yet 4 of them clearly ended without the customer's problem being solved. In 3 of 5 calls, the "Last inbound call state" shows "Abandoned in CSAT rating", meaning customers dropped off during the satisfaction rating prompt itself — a strong signal of dissatisfaction, not resolution.

Why this matters: If Fin resolves 20 calls but 7 of those "resolutions" are actually unresolved, the 65% CSAT denominator is inflated with false positives. The real resolved CSAT is likely even lower on genuinely-resolved calls — or alternatively, some low CSAT reflects calls that never should have counted as resolved.

One anomaly: Conv #215473617766020 (technician access) appears to be a genuinely successful call — the user confirmed every step. This 1/5 rating is suspect and may be a mis-key or CSAT system artifact (also flagged as a duplicate call).

🔧 Recommended Fixes
  • Audit Fin's resolution confirmation logic for voice: tighten the threshold so "user acknowledged next step" without solving the problem does not trigger "Confirmed Resolution"
  • Create a Custom Scorecard rule (ties to GTM-379): calls where Fin resolution state = confirmed but CSAT = 1 should auto-flag for QA review
  • Investigate whether the keypad-based CSAT prompt ("press 1–5") is misregistering input for calls that end abruptly — 3/5 calls show "Abandoned in CSAT rating" as final state
  • For Conv #215473617766020: manually review the call recording; if genuinely successful, exclude from failure analysis
Supporting Pattern #4
🔧 Third-Party Setup Gaps — Zillow / External Platform Confusion
Frequency: 1 of 5 calls (20%)

What's happening: User calling about Listing Syndication + Zillow integration asked for clarification on "linking the DoorLoop Company ID with Zillow to ensure automatic posting" — and walked away with procedural steps but uncertain about the final setup. Fin's answer was technically correct but incomplete for a caller doing this over the phone without a screen.

Why customers rate 1/5: Multi-step account integrations don't work well in a voice conversation. The customer "confirmed understanding" during the call but clearly didn't complete setup — hence the low rating.

🔧 Recommended Fixes
  • Add a Fin Procedure: for multi-step setup tasks (Listing Syndications, Zillow linking), offer to send a follow-up email with the Help Center article link after the call
  • Update the voice workflow to detect "setup / configuration" intent and offer email follow-up as a standard close

Assumptions & Limitations (Fact-Grounded)

What to know before acting on this analysis

  • Sample size is small: Only 5 conversations have ai_agent_rating = 1 in the Jan–Jun 2026 dataset. Patterns are directional, not statistically definitive.
  • No audio available: Analysis is based entirely on Intercom's auto-generated call_summary (AI-generated summaries of voice calls), not raw transcripts. Summary accuracy depends on Intercom's transcription quality.
  • No written CSAT remarks: All 5 calls have ai_agent_rating_remark: null. No customer-written explanations are available. Failure modes are inferred from call summaries and system attributes.
  • Resolution state may not match reality: All 5 calls show confirmed_resolution in Intercom — but 4 clearly did not resolve the customer's issue. This is a data quality issue, not a Fin content issue.
  • June 2026 "0% Fin AI CSAT" is a data artifact, not real performance: Verified by pulling all 2,368 conversations — June had 4 CSAT-rated calls and all 4 were 5/5. The 0% appears because ai_agent.rating (which tracks Fin-solo resolutions) had zero entries in June, not because customers were unhappy. This is a metric definition gap in the report display.
  • Fin Voice is relatively new: The report shows near-zero Fin involvement in Jan 2026 (0.04%), growing to 6.97% by June. The pattern may continue evolving as more calls are routed through Fin.
  • "Lead" vs "user" does not perfectly map to tenant vs PM: Intercom classifies callers as "lead" when they are not recognized in the system (no matching DoorLoop account). While most leads are likely tenants or applicants, some may be property managers calling from an unrecognized number. The 90.1% figure is a ceiling estimate of non-customer traffic, not a precise tenant count.
