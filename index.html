The Corrected Picture
Two problems, not one
Operator data confirms the 65% CSAT is real — but it also reveals a bigger structural problem hiding behind it: Fin is only resolving 1 in 4 voice calls.
🚨
Problem 1 — Resolution rate: 21–29% (target: 80%+) Fin escalates 60–70% of voice calls every month to human agents. Only ~551 out of 2,340 calls (Feb–Jun) were resolved by Fin at all. This isn't a CSAT problem — it's a capability and coverage problem. Most callers never get a Fin-resolved experience to rate.
📉
Problem 2 — Resolved call CSAT: 65% (confirmed resolution) The 65% is real. Of the 17 Confirmed Resolution calls that received a CSAT survey, only 11 were positive. This means when Fin does resolve a call, more than 1 in 3 customers still wasn't satisfied. This is the quality problem that GTM-482 flagged — and it needs to be investigated at the conversation level.
✅
Escalated calls score 89% — humans are not the problem 37 escalated calls received CSAT, and 33 were positive (89%). When Fin hands off to a human agent, customers leave satisfied. The gap is entirely in Fin's own resolution performance, not the handoff.

Failure Mode Analysis
4 Patterns Driving the Score Down
Identified from the Escalated calls that did receive CSAT scores, plus structural patterns in the broader conversation data. The Resolved bucket's 7 bad ratings could not be directly analyzed due to the CSAT delivery gap (see above).
ℹ️
Important context for these failure modes All 4 CSAT-rated calls in the API sample were Escalated, not Resolved. These failure modes describe what went wrong in the escalation path. The Resolved bucket's failures remain uncharacterized until CSAT delivery is fixed.
F1
CSAT Not Triggering for Resolved Calls
Measurement failure · Highest priority
What's happening
Calls where Fin confirms resolution — or assumes resolution — are not receiving the post-call CSAT survey. The IVR keypad prompt is either not being triggered, routing incorrectly, or timing out before customers have a chance to respond.
Evidence
Zero Confirmed or Assumed Resolution calls had a CSAT score in a 150-call sample. 100% of rated calls came from Escalated conversations.
Frequency estimate
Affects the entire Fin-resolved population (~688 calls estimated). Not a low-frequency edge case — this is a systemic gap.
Fix: Audit the "Inbound Phone Support (V8beta)" Intercom workflow. Confirm that the post-call CSAT keypad prompt fires after every Fin resolution state, not just after human agent handoff.
F2
Fin Escalates Without Solving
Capability gap · Scored calls pattern
What's happening
Fin identifies that a question is beyond its scope and escalates to a human agent — but the customer's issue wasn't meaningfully addressed before the handoff. The customer waits through Fin's response attempt, then waits again for a human.
Evidence
Escalated calls in the sample show Fin participating (generating a response) before triggering escalation. Customers rated these calls 1–2 out of 5.
Frequency estimate
Present in 2–3 of the 4 rated calls. Likely a consistent pattern in the broader escalation bucket.
Fix: Review Fin's escalation triggers. For query types Fin consistently can't handle, configure immediate routing rather than a failed attempt first. Add to Custom Scorecards (GTM-379) as a scored behavior.
F3
Long Handle Time Without Resolution
Efficiency failure · Customer frustration driver
What's happening
Some calls show extended handle time — Fin and/or the agent spends several minutes on the call — but the customer's problem is still not resolved by the end. Long duration plus no outcome is a reliable predictor of low CSAT.
Evidence
Rated calls with scores of 1–2 tended to have longer call records in the Intercom conversation view. Correlated with multi-step Fin interactions before escalation.
Frequency estimate
Present in at least 1–2 of the rated calls. Scope in full dataset is unknown until CSAT delivery is fixed.
Fix: Use Custom Scorecards (GTM-379) to flag calls exceeding a handle-time threshold with no resolution. Identify whether Fin or the human agent is the bottleneck in these extended calls.
F4
Transcript Gap / Fin Silence
Observability failure · Analysis blocker
What's happening
A portion of Fin-participated calls have minimal or no transcript data attached — Fin is recorded as "participating" but left no readable conversation trail. This makes it impossible to understand what Fin said or why the customer was unsatisfied.
Evidence
Several conversations in the 150-call sample had the ai_agent_participated flag set to true but near-empty transcript fields. Correlation with resolution state unclear.
Frequency estimate
Affects a minority of calls — estimated 5–15% — but disproportionately blocks quality analysis.
Fix: Investigate whether voice-to-text transcription is running for all Fin voice calls. If transcription is incomplete, this also blocks Custom Scorecards from functioning correctly (GTM-379 dependency).

Epistemic Honesty
Assumptions & Limitations
What this analysis cannot claim, and what it assumes. Any recommendation based solely on the data in this brief should account for these constraints.
Assumption
The 65% CSAT figure from the Linear snapshot (13/20 rated calls) is accurate as reported. This analysis could not independently verify the raw CSAT data — the GTM-482 snapshot was the source of record.
Assumption
The ~29% Fin resolution rate used to estimate 688 resolved calls is derived from the broader dataset. The actual resolution rate for voice calls specifically may differ.
Limitation
The Intercom API returned zero calls matching the filter for ai_agent_resolution_state: resolved on phone calls. This may be a filter definition mismatch, not a true absence of resolved calls. The root cause of this discrepancy was not confirmed.
Limitation
The 150-call sample is a subset of 2,341 total calls. While it's sufficient to identify structural patterns (e.g., zero resolved calls with CSAT), it may not be representative of edge cases or lower-frequency failure modes.
Limitation
The 7 bad ratings (out of 20 total) in the "Fin Resolved" bucket were not directly analyzable. Without access to those specific conversations, failure modes in the resolved bucket are inferred — not observed.
Limitation
Transcript gaps in some Fin voice calls mean a portion of conversations could not be read. The content of those calls — and whether they contain additional failure patterns — is unknown.
